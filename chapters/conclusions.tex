\section*{Conclusions and recommendations}

In this laboratory work, both the differential and integral methods were
applied to determine the reaction orders of the systems under investigation.
Problems 1 and 2 showed that the integral method provides suitable results for
standard reactions, but is limited to simple, integer reaction orders. For
Problem 3, where only one measurement series with a limited number of
irregularly spaced data points was available, the differential method was
used in combination with a fifth-degree polynomial interpolation. It is
important to note that using a fifth-degree polynomial as an approximation
can lead to overfitting and thus distort the results. In this case, however,
the calculations produced plausible results. The differential method makes it
possible to represent the reaction order more precisely than the integral
method does when assuming a first-order reaction. In addition, this
laboratory work demonstrated how temperature-dependent parameters
(activation energy) can be reliably calculated from experimentally
determined kinetic data.
